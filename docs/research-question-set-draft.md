# Research question set draft

Five topics, ten factual questions each. Answers are deliberately short; each item has a source opened during preparation unless marked otherwise.

## Topic 1 — U.S. data-center electricity and the grid

1. What share of U.S. electricity did data centers consume in 2023? **Answer:** About 4.4%. [DOE, verified by opening](https://www.energy.gov/articles/doe-releases-new-report-evaluating-increase-electricity-demand-data-centers)
2. How much electricity did U.S. data centers consume in 2014? **Answer:** About 58 TWh. [DOE, verified by opening](https://www.energy.gov/articles/doe-releases-new-report-evaluating-increase-electricity-demand-data-centers)
3. How much did they consume in 2023? **Answer:** About 176 TWh. [DOE, verified by opening](https://www.energy.gov/articles/doe-releases-new-report-evaluating-increase-electricity-demand-data-centers)
4. What is DOE’s 2028 data-center electricity range? **Answer:** 325–580 TWh. [DOE, verified by opening](https://www.energy.gov/articles/doe-releases-new-report-evaluating-increase-electricity-demand-data-centers)
5. What was annual U.S. electricity-demand growth from 2020 to 2025? **Answer:** About 1.7%. [EIA, verified by opening](https://www.eia.gov/todayinenergy/detail.php/detail.php?id=67344)
6. What was the comparable annual growth rate from 2005 to 2019? **Answer:** About 0.1%. [EIA, verified by opening](https://www.eia.gov/todayinenergy/detail.php/detail.php?id=67344)
7. Which two regions does EIA identify as having the fastest data-center-related load growth through 2027? **Answer:** ERCOT and PJM. [EIA, verified by opening](https://www.eia.gov/todayinenergy/detail.php/detail.php?id=67344)
8. What 2027 ERCOT wholesale-price increase does EIA model in its high-demand scenario? **Answer:** $37/MWh above the February STEO forecast. [EIA, verified by opening](https://www.eia.gov/todayinenergy/detail.php/detail.php?id=67344)
9. What two-year natural-gas generation increase does EIA forecast in the high-demand scenario? **Answer:** 7.3%, or 123 billion kWh. [EIA, verified by opening](https://www.eia.gov/todayinenergy/detail.php/detail.php?id=67344)
10. What is the arithmetic growth factor from 58 TWh to 176 TWh? **Answer:** About 3.03×, calculated as 176 ÷ 58. [DOE, verified by opening](https://www.energy.gov/articles/doe-releases-new-report-evaluating-increase-electricity-demand-data-centers)

## Topic 2 — Colorado River post-2026 operations

1. What year does the 2026 AOP cover? **Answer:** Calendar year 2026. [AOP, verified by opening](https://www.usbr.gov/lc/region/g4000/aop/AOP26.pdf)
2. Is the 2026 AOP the same document as the Post-2026 operating-guidelines negotiation? **Answer:** No; the AOP says it is separate. [AOP, verified by opening](https://www.usbr.gov/lc/region/g4000/aop/AOP26.pdf)
3. Which shortage framework does the AOP incorporate? **Answer:** The Colorado River Interim Guidelines for Lower Basin Shortages, among other instruments. [AOP, verified by opening](https://www.usbr.gov/lc/region/g4000/aop/AOP26.pdf)
4. Which drought-contingency framework does it incorporate? **Answer:** The Lower Basin Drought Contingency Plan Agreement. [AOP, verified by opening](https://www.usbr.gov/lc/region/g4000/aop/AOP26.pdf)
5. What does Reclamation’s Post-2026 page list for the 2027–28 period? **Answer:** 2027–28 operating guidelines. [Reclamation, verified by opening](https://www.usbr.gov/ColoradoRiverBasin/post2026/)
6. What longer planning period does the page list? **Answer:** A 2027–36 framework. [Reclamation, verified by opening](https://www.usbr.gov/ColoradoRiverBasin/post2026/)
7. What federal decision is listed alongside those documents? **Answer:** A Record of Decision. [Reclamation, verified by opening](https://www.usbr.gov/ColoradoRiverBasin/post2026/)
8. What kind of hydrologic conditions does the AOP say it uses for its projected plan? **Answer:** Most-probable runoff conditions. [AOP, verified by opening](https://www.usbr.gov/lc/region/g4000/aop/AOP26.pdf)
9. What statute does the AOP identify as the basis for coordinated long-range reservoir operation? **Answer:** The Colorado River Basin Project Act of September 30, 1968. [AOP, verified by opening](https://www.usbr.gov/lc/region/g4000/aop/AOP26.pdf)
10. What is the correct first step when comparing a proposed post-2026 cut with the AOP? **Answer:** Identify whether each number is an adopted rule, an operating projection, or a proposal before calculating the difference. [AOP and Reclamation, verified by opening](https://www.usbr.gov/lc/region/g4000/aop/AOP26.pdf)

## Topic 3 — 2025 Arctic sea ice

1. What does NOAA’s 2025 sea-ice page report for the March monthly average? **Answer:** 14.12 million km². [NOAA, verified by opening](https://arctic.noaa.gov/report-card/report-card-2025/sea-ice-2025/)
2. What does it report for the September monthly average? **Answer:** 4.75 million km². [NOAA, verified by opening](https://arctic.noaa.gov/report-card/report-card-2025/sea-ice-2025/)
3. What long-run September trend does NOAA list for 1979–2025? **Answer:** −76.1 thousand km² per year. [NOAA, verified by opening](https://arctic.noaa.gov/report-card/report-card-2025/sea-ice-2025/)
4. What long-run March trend does NOAA list? **Answer:** −38 thousand km² per year. [NOAA, verified by opening](https://arctic.noaa.gov/report-card/report-card-2025/sea-ice-2025/)
5. What is the difference between NOAA’s March and September 2025 monthly averages? **Answer:** 9.37 million km², calculated as 14.12 − 4.75. [NOAA, verified by opening](https://arctic.noaa.gov/report-card/report-card-2025/sea-ice-2025/)
6. What is that September average as a percentage of the March average? **Answer:** About 33.6%, calculated as 4.75 ÷ 14.12 × 100. [NOAA, verified by opening](https://arctic.noaa.gov/report-card/report-card-2025/sea-ice-2025/)
7. What kind of record does NOAA say March 2025 set? **Answer:** The lowest annual maximum in the 47-year satellite record. [NOAA, verified by opening](https://arctic.noaa.gov/report-card/report-card-2025/sea-ice-2025/)
8. What age class of old ice does NOAA highlight for September 2025? **Answer:** Ice older than four years. [NOAA, verified by opening](https://arctic.noaa.gov/report-card/report-card-2025/sea-ice-2025/)
9. Why can two official sea-ice minima differ without one being fraudulent? **Answer:** They may use different dates, averaging windows, grids, and spatial resolutions. [NOAA context, verified by opening](https://arctic.noaa.gov/report-card/report-card-2025/)
10. Which agency’s comparison page should be checked for the USNIC value? **Answer:** The National Ice Center’s official release. [USNIC link, not opened in this pass](https://usnicweb2.espc.nesdis.noaa.gov/PressRelease/ArcticMinimumMaximum2025_2026)

## Topic 4 — Cement and concrete decarbonization

1. What happened to total cement-and-concrete emissions relative to 2015, according to IEA? **Answer:** They are higher today. [IEA, verified by opening](https://www.iea.org/reports/breakthrough-agenda-report-2025/cement-and-concrete)
2. What happened to direct emissions intensity? **Answer:** It remained unchanged. [IEA, verified by opening](https://www.iea.org/reports/breakthrough-agenda-report-2025/cement-and-concrete)
3. Name one pathway family IEA identifies. **Answer:** Material efficiency. [IEA, verified by opening](https://www.iea.org/reports/breakthrough-agenda-report-2025/cement-and-concrete)
4. Name a second pathway family. **Answer:** Alternative fuels. [IEA, verified by opening](https://www.iea.org/reports/breakthrough-agenda-report-2025/cement-and-concrete)
5. Name a third pathway family. **Answer:** Supplementary cementitious materials. [IEA, verified by opening](https://www.iea.org/reports/breakthrough-agenda-report-2025/cement-and-concrete)
6. Name a fourth pathway family. **Answer:** Carbon capture and storage. [IEA, verified by opening](https://www.iea.org/reports/breakthrough-agenda-report-2025/cement-and-concrete)
7. What cost premium range does IEA give for near-zero-emissions CCS plants? **Answer:** 75–150% higher. [IEA, verified by opening](https://www.iea.org/reports/breakthrough-agenda-report-2025/cement-and-concrete)
8. If a baseline plant costs 100 units, what range does that premium imply? **Answer:** 175–250 units, before other assumptions. [IEA, verified by opening](https://www.iea.org/reports/breakthrough-agenda-report-2025/cement-and-concrete)
9. Why is “cement” not identical to “concrete” in a research question? **Answer:** Cement is a binding ingredient; concrete is the composite product using cement plus aggregates and water. [IEA topic framing, verified by opening](https://www.iea.org/reports/breakthrough-agenda-report-2025/cement-and-concrete)
10. What is the central trend challenge identified by the IEA facts above? **Answer:** Emissions have not fallen fast enough despite available pathway families. [IEA, verified by opening](https://www.iea.org/reports/breakthrough-agenda-report-2025/cement-and-concrete)

## Topic 5 — The Hubble tension

1. What local Hubble-constant range does NASA summarize? **Answer:** Roughly 70–76 km/s/Mpc. [NASA, verified by opening](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/hubble/science/science-behind-the-discoveries/hubble-constant-and-tension/)
2. What CMB-inferred range does it summarize? **Answer:** Roughly 67–68 km/s/Mpc. [NASA, verified by opening](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/hubble/science/science-behind-the-discoveries/hubble-constant-and-tension/)
3. What is the midpoint of the local range? **Answer:** 73 km/s/Mpc. [NASA range, verified by opening](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/hubble/science/science-behind-the-discoveries/hubble-constant-and-tension/)
4. What is the midpoint of the CMB range? **Answer:** 67.5 km/s/Mpc. [NASA range, verified by opening](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/hubble/science/science-behind-the-discoveries/hubble-constant-and-tension/)
5. What is the midpoint gap? **Answer:** 5.5 km/s/Mpc, calculated as 73 − 67.5. [NASA, verified by opening](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/hubble/science/science-behind-the-discoveries/hubble-constant-and-tension/)
6. What is that gap relative to the CMB midpoint? **Answer:** About 8.1%, calculated as 5.5 ÷ 67.5 × 100. [NASA, verified by opening](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/hubble/science/science-behind-the-discoveries/hubble-constant-and-tension/)
7. What broad method produces the local inference? **Answer:** The distance ladder. [NASA, verified by opening](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/hubble/science/science-behind-the-discoveries/hubble-constant-and-tension/)
8. What broad observation produces the early-universe inference? **Answer:** The cosmic microwave background. [NASA, verified by opening](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/hubble/science/science-behind-the-discoveries/hubble-constant-and-tension/)
9. What age does NASA give for the universe in this context? **Answer:** About 13.8 billion years. [NASA, verified by opening](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/hubble/science/science-behind-the-discoveries/hubble-constant-and-tension/)
10. What are the two top-level explanations a report should compare? **Answer:** Remaining measurement/systematic error versus a change to the cosmological model or new physics. [NASA framing, verified by opening](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/hubble/science/science-behind-the-discoveries/hubble-constant-and-tension/)
